Predicting Partially Masked Judicial Votes on European Court of Human Rights Panels

Data Science Capstone (ISTE 470) | Rochester Institute of Technology

Problem

Some procedural ballots conceal their outcomes, and most masked test ballots involve respondent countries absent from training.

My contribution

Built a panel-level prediction pipeline combining dissent indicators, empirical Bayes judge priors, procedural features, and a three-model ensemble.

Result

Produced a Python reconstruction pipeline and an IEEE capstone report. The supplied summary reports 96.266% holdout accuracy and 69.03% country-held-out accuracy.

The report and validation artifacts are not linked here. Overall holdout accuracy includes observable merits ballots; the reported masked-ballot accuracy is 83.16%. Country-held-out and significance results require supporting experiment artifacts.

Technologies & methods
Tools: Python, scikit-learn, pandas, NumPy, Empirical Bayes, TF-IDF, Gradient Boosting

Technical details

This capstone project investigates judicial behavior and cross-border generalization across 10,000 ballots (7,000 training, 3,000 test) cast on multi-judge European Court of Human Rights (ECtHR) panels. While approximately 75% of the dataset consists of substantive merits rulings with directly observable verdicts (y_i = 1 - violation_vote_i), the remaining 25% (1,818 training and 665 test ballots) covers secondary procedural issues, such as admissibility, damages, and docket strikes, where the outcome label is administratively masked and 88.9% of masked test ballots involve 15 unseen respondent countries. Rather than treating judges as interchangeable text processors, the study tests whether procedural rules, voting coalitions, and national allegiance govern judicial decisions.

  • Zero-Contradiction Panel Modeling: Collapsed masked ballots into panel-level equilibria (y_i = M_p XOR d_i) across 126 training and 51 test panels, reducing target degrees of freedom by 13x and eliminating intra-panel voting contradictions.
  • Cross-Border Judge & Coalition Features: Estimated leak-free Empirical Bayes judge ideology priors across 7,517 disjoint unmasked merits ballots (N_overlap = 0), quantified national-judge bias shifts (+37.91% pro-government shift on home-country merits cases versus -24.64% on procedural dismissals), and combined 28 structural/clause-parsing features with 450 TF-IDF n-grams in an equally weighted three-model ensemble.
  • Validation, Diagnostics & Deliverables: Enforced strict Leave-One-Country-Out (LOGO) validation to raise cold-start accuracy on unseen countries from 57.04% to 69.03% (p = 0.0007), diagnosed a Bayesian shrinkage covariate shift on three Grand Chamber panels, and achieved a 0.96266 holdout leaderboard score (83.16% on masked test ballots) alongside a reproducible Python pipeline and IEEE research paper.

Orthodox Parish Data Warehouse & Spatial Analytics Platform

Problem

Independent church directories overlap and use inconsistent formats, making geographic and hierarchical analysis difficult.

My contribution

Designed an Airflow ingestion pipeline, distance-based entity resolution, a MySQL spatial star schema, and a separate GeoJSON API.

Result

Connects jurisdiction records to viewport queries, canonical hierarchy summaries, and Voronoi parish territories.

Technologies & methods
Apache Airflow / Python / MySQL 8.0 (Spatial & Star Schema) / Node.js / Express / GeoJSON / Vite

Technical details

An end-to-end spatial data warehouse and interactive mapping platform tracking Eastern Orthodox parishes, monasteries, and episcopal sees across North America and Europe. Orchestrated with Apache Airflow, the pipeline ingests data from 20 jurisdiction scrapers, resolves duplicates via spherical distance and fuzzy string matching, and populates a MySQL 8.0 Star Schema to serve real-time GeoJSON viewport queries and Voronoi parish territories to a decoupled web client.

Directory data for Eastern Orthodox parishes is fragmented across independent patriarchal, archdiocesan, and aggregator websites with inconsistent formatting and overlapping listings. The platform separates ingestion, analytical storage, and presentation into three independent tiers.

An Apache Airflow DAG uses Dynamic Task Mapping to run 20 jurisdiction-specific extractors in parallel, landing raw records into MySQL staging. Transformations standardize clerical titles and addresses, incrementally geocode hashed location keys, and deduplicate entities using source-trust weighting, ST_Distance_Sphere proximity, and token similarity. Validated records load into a Kimball Star Schema with fact_parish_snapshot and six dimensions, SRID 4326 spatial indexes, precomputed Voronoi catchment polygons, and WITH ROLLUP OLAP views. A stateless Node.js REST API uses MBRContains bounding-box filters to deliver lightweight GeoJSON to a standalone SPA.

  • ETL Orchestration: Apache Airflow Dynamic Task Mapping extracts, sanitizes, and incrementally geocodes records across 20 North American and European scrapers.
  • Dimensional Modeling: MySQL 8.0 Star Schema with fact_parish_snapshot, six dimensions, SRID 4326 spatial indexes, and WITH ROLLUP aggregations across canonical hierarchies.
  • Entity Resolution: Spherical distance (ST_Distance_Sphere), fuzzy token matching, and source-trust weighting reconcile overlapping diocesan and aggregator directories.
  • Service Decoupling: A stateless Node.js GeoJSON API replaces static JSON snapshots, serving MBRContains viewport queries and Voronoi territory polygons to a standalone frontend.
